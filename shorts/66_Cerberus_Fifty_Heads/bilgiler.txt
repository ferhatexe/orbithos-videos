AÇIKLAMA:
Cerberus, the guard dog of the underworld, has three heads — but in Hesiod's Theogony, one of the oldest Greek poems (around 700 BC), he is fifty-headed and bronze-voiced. His brother was Orthrus, a two-headed dog, and his sister was the Hydra. The poet Pindar went further and gave him a hundred heads. Artists settled for fewer: on Greek vases he usually has two, and later writers fixed it at three.

His job was strange too: he welcomed the dead into the underworld, and ate anyone who tried to leave. Heracles dragged him up to the living world as his final labor, Orpheus put him to sleep with music, and in Virgil's Aeneid the Sibyl tossed him a drugged honey cake — the origin of the old English phrase "a sop to Cerberus": a small bribe to keep trouble quiet.
