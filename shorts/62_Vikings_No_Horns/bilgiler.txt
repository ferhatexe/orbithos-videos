AÇIKLAMA:
Only one nearly complete Viking Age helmet has ever been found: the Gjermundbu helmet, dug up on a farm in Norway in 1943. It's a rounded iron cap with a spectacle-like guard around the eyes — and no horns. In battle, horns would only have given an enemy's blade something to catch.

So where did the horns come from? Nineteenth-century romantic painters started it, and in 1876 costume designer Carl Emil Doepler put horned helmets on stage for the first Bayreuth production of Wagner's Ring cycle. The opera was a sensation, and the look stuck.

Horned helmets did exist in Scandinavia — but in the Bronze Age. The Veksø helmets from Denmark date to around 900 BC, roughly 1,700 years before the Vikings, and were probably ceremonial.
