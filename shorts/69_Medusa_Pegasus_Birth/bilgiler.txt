AÇIKLAMA:
In Hesiod's Theogony, Medusa was one of three Gorgon sisters — and the only one who could die. Her sisters, Stheno and Euryale, were immortal. In later tellings, Perseus cut off her head while she slept, looking at her only in a reflection; Hermes gave him a curved blade, and Athena guided his hand.

From her severed neck sprang two sons of Poseidon: the winged horse Pegasus and the warrior Chrysaor. Pegasus later carried thunder and lightning for Zeus. Even dead, her gaze still worked — Perseus used the head as a weapon, and in Ovid, seaweed it touched hardened into coral. Athena later placed the head on her shield, and Greeks painted that face on shields and buildings to scare evil away. Ovid also rewrote Medusa as a beautiful woman cursed by Athena.
