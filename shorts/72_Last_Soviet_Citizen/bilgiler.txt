AÇIKLAMA:
In May 1991, cosmonaut Sergei Krikalev launched from Baikonur to the Mir space station for a mission of about five months. While he was in orbit, his country began to unravel: in August, tanks rolled into Moscow during a failed coup, and his hometown of Leningrad took back the name Saint Petersburg.

With money running short, flights were cut, and the seat that would have carried his replacement went to a Kazakh guest cosmonaut flying alongside a paying Austrian researcher. Krikalev agreed to stay. In December 1991 the Soviet Union dissolved while he was still circling the planet — and the Baikonur launch site now belonged to an independent Kazakhstan.

He landed on March 25, 1992, after about 311 days in space, roughly twice as long as planned. Two years later he became the first Russian cosmonaut to fly on an American Space Shuttle (STS-60), and in 2000 he was part of the first crew of the International Space Station. He is often called "the last citizen of the Soviet Union."
