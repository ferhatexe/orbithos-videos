AÇIKLAMA:
In Greek myth, Zeus had a son with a mortal woman: Heracles — whose name, ironically, means "glory of Hera." Zeus wanted the baby to become immortal, and for that he needed divine milk. So the infant was placed at the breast of Hera, Zeus's wife, while she slept.

Hera woke up and realized this was not her child. She pushed him away, and her milk sprayed across the night sky. The drops became stars — a glowing river of light. Centuries later, Tintoretto (c. 1575) and Rubens (1636–37) painted the moment.

The Greeks called that band of light "galaxias kyklos," the milky circle, from "gala," milk. The Romans called it "Via Lactea," the Milky Way. And when astronomers discovered other star systems far beyond our own, they named them all galaxies.
