AÇIKLAMA:
In Homer's Iliad, Achilles is a terrifying warrior — but not invulnerable. In Book 21, the spear of Asteropaeus even grazes his arm and draws blood. And the poem ends before he dies; later writers say Paris killed him with an arrow.

The heel story first shows up centuries later, in the Roman poet Statius's unfinished Achilleid, in the first century AD: his mother Thetis dipped baby Achilles in the river Styx, holding him by one heel, which stayed dry. In later retellings, the fatal arrow strikes exactly there. An older version had her burning away his mortality with fire.

The tendon was first recorded as named after Achilles in 1693, by the Flemish anatomist Philip Verheyen, and "Achilles' heel" as a word for weakness only caught on in the 1800s.
