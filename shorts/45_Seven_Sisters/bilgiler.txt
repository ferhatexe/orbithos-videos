AÇIKLAMA:
In Greek myth, the Pleiades are the seven daughters of the titan Atlas and the sea-nymph Pleione. The hunter Orion chased them across the land until the gods placed them in the sky as stars. Yet only six are easy to see — and the Greeks explained why: Merope married a mortal, Sisyphus, and hid her face in shame, so her star is faint.

On the other side of the world, many Aboriginal Australian stories describe the stars of Orion as a man pursuing seven sisters across the sky — and again, one sister is hiding, lost or too young to be seen.

In 2021, astrophysicists Ray and Barnaby Norris ran the motions of the Pleiades backward. About 100,000 years ago, the stars Atlas and Pleione were further apart, and most people would have seen seven stars. It's only a hypothesis, but it raises a remarkable possibility: the "lost sister" could be one of the oldest stories humans have ever told.
