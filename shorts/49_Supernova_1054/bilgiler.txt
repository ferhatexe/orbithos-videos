AÇIKLAMA:
On July 4, 1054, astronomers of China's Song dynasty recorded a "guest star" near the constellation Taurus. It was as bright as Venus and visible in broad daylight for about 23 days, and it stayed visible at night for nearly two years before fading away. In the American Southwest, people may have painted it on a rock — a famous pictograph shows a hand, a crescent moon and a star.

It was a supernova: a giant star exploding at the end of its life. For a few weeks, a single exploding star can outshine its entire galaxy. Its light took about 6,500 years to reach Earth.

Today, telescopes see the wreckage — the Crab Nebula, still expanding. At its heart is a neutron star about the size of a city, spinning 30 times every second and sweeping beams across space like a cosmic lighthouse.
