AÇIKLAMA:
In December 1995, astronomers pointed the Hubble Space Telescope at a patch of sky near the Big Dipper that looked completely empty — a spot about the size of a pinhead held at arm's length. Some colleagues called it a waste of precious telescope time.

For ten straight days, Hubble stared at it, taking 342 exposures. Then the image came back: almost every speck of light in it was not a star but an entire galaxy — about 3,000 of them. Spirals, blobs and smudges, each holding billions of stars, with light that had been traveling for billions of years.

In 2004, the Hubble Ultra Deep Field looked even deeper and found around 10,000 galaxies. And that patch wasn't special: point anywhere, and the sky is just as crowded.
