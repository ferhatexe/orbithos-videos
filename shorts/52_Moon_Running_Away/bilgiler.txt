AÇIKLAMA:
The Moon is slowly drifting away from Earth — about 3.8 centimeters every year, roughly as fast as your fingernails grow. We know because in 1969, Apollo 11 astronauts left a small panel of mirrors on the Moon. Scientists fire lasers at it from Earth and catch the light bouncing back; the round trip takes about two and a half seconds, and timing it precisely reveals the Moon slipping away.

The cause is tides. The Moon pulls Earth's oceans into bulges. Because Earth spins faster than the Moon orbits, those bulges run ahead and tug the Moon forward. The Moon gains energy and climbs into a higher orbit, while Earth's spin slowly brakes.

About 1.4 billion years ago, a day on Earth lasted only around 18 hours. And in the far future, the Moon will look too small to fully cover the Sun — total solar eclipses will come to an end.
