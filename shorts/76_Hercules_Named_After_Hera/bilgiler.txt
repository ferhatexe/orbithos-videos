AÇIKLAMA:
Heracles — Hercules to the Romans — is the greatest hero of Greek myth. His name is usually read as "glory of Hera" (Hēra + kleos). Yet Hera, the wife of Zeus, hated him from before he was born, because he was Zeus's son with a mortal woman, Alcmene. In Homer's Iliad she holds back his birth so that his rival Eurystheus, born at seven months, wins the throne. When Heracles was eight months old (Apollodorus), she sent two serpents into his cradle — and the baby strangled one with each hand.

He wasn't even born "Heracles": the sources call him Alcides or Alcaeus, after his grandfather Alcaeus. In one version (Diodorus Siculus), the people of Argos renamed him after the serpents, because he had won glory through Hera. In another (Apollodorus), the Pythia at Delphi gave him the name after Hera sent the madness that cost him his family — and as penance he served Eurystheus for twelve years: the famous labours.

At the end of his life he rose to Olympus as a god and was reconciled with Hera. Diodorus even says she adopted him, and he married her daughter Hebe, goddess of youth.
