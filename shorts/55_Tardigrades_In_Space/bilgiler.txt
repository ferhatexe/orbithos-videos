AÇIKLAMA:
Tardigrades — "water bears" — are microscopic animals about half a millimeter long, with eight stubby clawed legs. They live almost everywhere, from mountaintops to the deep sea. When conditions turn bad, they dry out, curl into a ball called a "tun," and slow their metabolism almost to zero.

In September 2007, ESA's FOTON-M3 mission carried dried tardigrades into low Earth orbit and exposed them directly to space for ten days: vacuum and cosmic radiation. Back on Earth, scientists added water. Many woke up, and some even laid eggs that hatched.

In April 2019, the Israeli lander Beresheet crashed on the Moon carrying thousands of dehydrated tardigrades. Did they survive the impact? Nobody knows. But without water, they can't wake up.
