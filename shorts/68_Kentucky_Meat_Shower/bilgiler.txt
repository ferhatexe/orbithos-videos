On March 3rd, 1876, chunks of meat fell from a clear sky in Bath County, Kentucky, near Olympia Springs. A woman making soap in her yard watched it come down. The pieces covered a strip about 100 yards long and 50 wide; most were around two inches across, a few bigger. The New York Times and Scientific American both covered it.

Two local men tasted it and said it was mutton or venison. Scientists who examined samples found lung tissue, muscle and cartilage. One early theory blamed nostoc, a jelly-like bacterium that swells in rain — but it hadn't rained.

The favorite explanation today: a flock of vultures throwing up their meal in mid-air. Vultures really do this, to get light enough for a quick escape.
