AÇIKLAMA:
In Greek myth, Atlas was a Titan who fought against Zeus and lost. His punishment: to stand at the western edge of the world and hold up the sky — forever. Not the planet, the heavens. Heracles once tricked him into taking the sky back, and in another myth, Perseus used Medusa's head to turn him to stone, creating the Atlas Mountains.

The mix-up comes from statues like the Farnese Atlas, a Roman marble almost two thousand years old. He carries a giant ball, but look closely: it is covered in constellations, making it one of the oldest surviving maps of the sky.

In 1595, a book of Gerardus Mercator's maps was titled "Atlas," and the name stuck. Even the Atlantic Ocean is named after him — the sea of Atlas.
