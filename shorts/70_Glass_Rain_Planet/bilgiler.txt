AÇIKLAMA:
HD 189733 b is a "hot Jupiter" about 64 light-years away, discovered in 2005. It orbits its star so closely that a year there lasts just over two days, and its atmosphere reaches around 1,000 °C.

In 2013, Hubble measured its color: a deep cobalt blue. Not oceans — scientists think the blue comes from high, hazy clouds full of silicate particles, the material glass is made of. With winds of roughly 4,500 mph (about 7,000 km/h), those particles could be falling as glass rain, blown sideways. The glass rain is a well-supported hypothesis, not a direct observation.

In 2024, the James Webb Space Telescope detected hydrogen sulfide in its air — the gas that gives rotten eggs their smell — the first time the molecule was found on a planet outside our solar system.
