AÇIKLAMA:
Stars forge new elements in their cores, but only up to iron — beyond iron, fusion stops paying off. Heavier atoms need a sudden flood of neutrons, faster than anything can decay. One place that happens: when two neutron stars collide. Each one is a city-sized ball, heavier than our Sun.

In August 2017, LIGO and Virgo detected gravitational waves from exactly that — a crash about 130 million light-years away (GW170817). Telescopes then watched the glowing debris, a kilonova, and found the fingerprints of freshly made heavy elements; by one estimate, about five Earth-masses of strontium alone. Gold, platinum and uranium are made in the same violent way, long before our Sun was born.
