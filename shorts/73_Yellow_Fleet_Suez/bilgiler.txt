AÇIKLAMA:
In June 1967, a convoy of fifteen merchant ships was sailing through the Suez Canal when the Six-Day War broke out between Israel and Egypt. The canal was sealed at both ends. Fourteen ships were trapped in the middle, in the Great Bitter Lake; the fifteenth waited alone further north in Lake Timsah.

Year after year, desert sand piled up on their decks, and they became known as the "Yellow Fleet." Their crews came from eight countries on both sides of the Cold War — Britain, West Germany, Sweden, Poland, Bulgaria, Czechoslovakia, France and the United States — and in October 1967 they founded the Great Bitter Lake Association. They shared food and spare parts, held church services on the German ship Nordwind, and in 1968 even staged their own Olympics, with sailing, diving and archery. Poland won; West Germany came second and Britain third. They printed their own stamps, which had no official validity — yet some letters still made it home.

The canal reopened in 1975. Only two ships, the West German Münsterland and Nordwind, could sail home under their own power, and thousands of people welcomed them in Hamburg.
