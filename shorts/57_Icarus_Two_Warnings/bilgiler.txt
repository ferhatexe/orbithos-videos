Icarus Was Also Warned Not to Fly Too Low ☀️🌊
AÇIKLAMA:
In Ovid's Metamorphoses, the inventor Daedalus and his son Icarus are held on Crete by King Minos. To escape, Daedalus builds wings: he lays feathers in rows from short to long, like a shepherd's panpipes, binds them in the middle with thread and fixes the base with wax.

Before they fly, he gives two warnings: don't fly too high, or the sun will melt the wax — and don't fly too low, or the sea will soak the feathers and drag you down. Fly the middle path. A fisherman and a shepherd see them overhead and think they are gods.

Icarus, thrilled, climbs toward the sun. The wax softens, the feathers slip, and he falls into the sea that bears his name, the Icarian Sea. Daedalus buries him on the nearest island, Icaria. We usually tell it as a warning against ambition — but in Ovid's telling, flying too low was just as deadly.
