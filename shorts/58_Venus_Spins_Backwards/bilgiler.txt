AÇIKLAMA:
Venus spins backwards compared to most planets, and incredibly slowly: one full rotation takes about 243 Earth days, while one orbit around the Sun takes about 225. So a single spin lasts longer than its entire year.

The twist: because it turns backwards as it orbits, the time from one sunrise to the next is only about 117 Earth days. Venus still gets almost two sunrises a year — and each one happens in the west. Sunset, about two months later, is in the east.

Meanwhile, the upper clouds race around the whole planet in roughly four days, and the surface sits at about 460 °C, hot enough to melt lead. Why Venus spins backwards is still debated: giant impacts and atmospheric tides are leading ideas, but nobody knows for sure.
