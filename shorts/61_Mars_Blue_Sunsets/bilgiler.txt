Sunsets on Mars Are Blue 🔵
AÇIKLAMA:
On Mars, the daytime sky is a dusty orange — but the sunsets are blue. NASA's Curiosity rover captured one on 15 April 2015 from Gale Crater.

On Earth, air molecules scatter blue light all across the sky, so our days are blue. At sunset, sunlight crosses so much air that the blue is scattered away, leaving reds and oranges. Mars has less than one percent of Earth's air, so the gas barely scatters anything. Instead, fine dust hangs in the sky and tints the day orange — but the grains are just the right size to send blue light forward, close to the direction of the Sun.

So at sunset, a cool blue glow gathers around the Sun — an Earth sunset in reverse. The Sun looks smaller there, about two-thirds as wide, and high dust keeps the twilight glowing for up to two hours.
