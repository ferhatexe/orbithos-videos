In April 1815, Mount Tambora in Indonesia exploded in the largest volcanic eruption in recorded history. Tens of thousands of people died from the blast and the famine that followed, and its ash and gas spread around the planet, dimming the sun.

The next year, 1816, became the "Year Without a Summer." It snowed in New England in June, frosts killed crops, Europe went hungry, and people nicknamed it "Eighteen Hundred and Froze to Death." Across Europe, sunsets glowed a strange, fiery red.

In Switzerland, endless storms kept a group of young writers indoors at the Villa Diodati on Lake Geneva. Lord Byron challenged them to write the best ghost story. Eighteen-year-old Mary Shelley dreamed up a scientist who builds a man — Frankenstein. The same contest produced John Polidori's "The Vampyre," the first modern vampire story.
