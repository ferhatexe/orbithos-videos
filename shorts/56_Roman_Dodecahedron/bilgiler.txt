AÇIKLAMA:
It's called a Roman dodecahedron: a small hollow bronze object with twelve pentagonal faces, a round hole in every face, and a knob on every corner. They're roughly 4 to 11 centimeters across, and the holes range from about 6 to 40 millimeters — each one a little different.

About 130 have been found, from northern England to Hungary, most made between the 2nd and 4th centuries AD. Some came from graves, others from coin hoards, as if they were valuables. Yet no surviving Roman text or picture describes them.

Candle holder, gaming die, measuring tool, religious object — all are guesses, and none is proven. In June 2023, volunteers of the Norton Disney History and Archaeology Group in Lincolnshire found one in almost perfect condition. It's beautiful, and still a mystery.
